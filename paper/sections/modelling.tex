\subsection{Experimental design}
\label{subsec:modelling_design}

Three feature sets are evaluated: AE-only, US-only, and combined AE+US. Both model families are fitted to each, giving six model--sensor configurations in total.

\subsubsection{Grouping and data splits}
The segments from which features are calculated are grouped to prevent soft leakage of information: segments collected close together in time or under matching operating conditions encode near-duplicate signal characteristics and must not straddle a fold boundary. The grouping protocol consists of two steps:
\begin{itemize}
  \item All segments recorded during the same 60\,s operating-condition hold are grouped together.
  \item Groups that share the same mean RPM and temperature \todo{Check that it is mean and what the thresholds are} are merged into a single group.
\end{itemize}
This yields \todo{Insert number of groups} groups of segments. The groups undergo an 80/20 group-level holdout split: 20\% of groups form a fixed holdout set evaluated once after all modelling decisions are finalised; the remaining 80\% constitute the training pool.

The training pool undergoes a single-pass 5-fold grouped cross-validation. Each fold trains and tunes a model independently on its 4/5 partition and evaluates once on the disjoint 1/5 holdout fold; predictions from all five holdout folds are pooled for significance testing. This procedure is visualised in Figure~\ref{fig:data_partition}.

\subsection{Model families}
\label{subsec:model_families}

Two regression model families are evaluated across the three sensor configurations,
as summarised in Table~\ref{tab:model_families}. For LightGBM, each CV fold contains
its own inner Optuna optimisation loop, ensuring that the reported fold scores are not
contaminated by hyperparameter selection on the fold test data. For Elastic Net,
\texttt{ElasticNetCV} performs an inner 4-fold grouped search over the
$\alpha$/$\ell_1$-ratio grid within each outer training partition; the selected
hyperparameters are refit on the full outer-training fold before the fold test set
is scored. The inner search is group-aware, matching the outer split structure.

\subsection{Statistical significance testing}
\label{subsec:sig_tests}

Three cross-sensor comparisons form the study's focal claims: AE vs.\ US,
Combined vs.\ AE, and Combined vs.\ US. Each is tested with a group-level
modified Diebold--Mariano test~\cite{harvey_testing_1997}. The unit of analysis
is the acquisition-hold group rather than the individual window: windows within
a 60\,s hold are near-duplicates, so a window-level test treats correlated
observations as independent and grossly overstates significance. Instead, the
squared-error differential $d_i = e_{A,i}^2 - e_{B,i}^2$ is averaged within
each holdout group, and the Harvey et al.\ small-sample correction
($\times\sqrt{(T-1)/T}$, $t_{T-1}$ reference distribution) is applied to the
resulting $T$ group means. The naive window-level $p$-value is reported
alongside each result to make the degree of inflation explicit.

\textbf{Overall performance.}
LightGBM achieves the best holdout performance across all sensor configurations,
with AE-only reaching $R^2 = \resHOrsqLgbAe$, RMSE~$= \resHOrmseLgbAe$~$\kappa$-units.
On a target range of $[\resKappaMin,\, \resKappaMax]$ with mean~\resKappaMean, this
corresponds to a relative RMSE of approximately \resRelRmsePctLgbAe\% of the mean
target value. Elastic Net on AE features achieves $R^2 = \resHOrsqEnetAe$, confirming
a non-linear feature--$\kappa$ mapping that gradient boosting exploits more
effectively. LightGBM consistently outperforms Elastic Net across all three sensor
configurations, confirming a non-linear feature--$\kappa$ mapping.

\textbf{Sensor comparison.}
AE features substantially outperform US features across both model families.
The LightGBM AE--US gap ($\Delta R^2 = \resDrsqLgbAeUs$) is large and confirmed
by the group-level DM test (Table~\ref{tab:inference}). This reflects the richer frequency
content and more direct physical sensitivity of the AE sensor to asperity contact
events.

\textbf{Sensor fusion.}
LightGBM on the combined AE+US feature set achieves $R^2 = \resHOrsqLgbComb$
($\Delta R^2 = \resDrsqLgbCombAe$ over the AE-only result of \resHOrsqLgbAe).
The gain is confirmed by the group-level DM test (Table~\ref{tab:inference}). The Elastic Net combined set also outperforms AE alone
($R^2$ \resHOrsqEnetComb{} vs.\ \resHOrsqEnetAe). Although the absolute $R^2$ gain
is modest, this is a relative RMSE reduction of about \resRelRmseRedCombAe\% over
AE alone, consistent across all four tests, indicating that the US channel
contributes genuine, statistically robust non-redundant information.

\textbf{Model consistency.}
The close agreement between CV RMSE and holdout RMSE across all models indicates
stable generalisation with no evidence of significant overfitting.

Figure~\ref{fig:pred_vs_actual} shows predicted vs.\ actual $\kappa$ on the holdout
set for both model families and all sensor configurations, coloured by RPM.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/model_pred_vs_actual_holdout.png}
\caption{Predicted vs.\ actual $\kappa$ on the holdout test set for both model families
and all sensor configurations, coloured by RPM. The dashed diagonal indicates perfect
prediction. Residual spread increases in the mixed lubrication regime
($0.5 \leq \kappa < 1.0$).}
\label{fig:pred_vs_actual}
\end{figure}

Across the grid, predictions track the diagonal most closely for LightGBM---tightest
in the AE and combined configurations---whereas Elastic Net systematically
under-predicts at high $\kappa$, its points bowing below the diagonal. For every
model the residual spread widens in the mixed-lubrication regime
($0.5 \leq \kappa < 1.0$) and toward the under-represented full-film range
($\kappa > 1$); the RPM colouring reflects the operating-point dependence of $\kappa$
(Section~\ref{subsec:confound}).

\textbf{SHAP-based feature importance.}
SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values computed via TreeExplainer identify
which features drive the $\kappa$ predictions for each sensor
(Figure~\ref{fig:shap}). For the AE LightGBM model the dominant contributor by a
large margin is \textit{\resShapTopFeat} (mean $|\text{SHAP}| = \resShapTopVal$),
followed by \textit{\resShapSecondFeat} (\resShapSecondVal) and
\textit{\resShapThirdFeat} (\resShapThirdVal); this 500~kHz--1~MHz mobility feature
ranks consistently among the top predictors across both model families. The US model
is led instead by \textit{\resShapTopFeatUs} (\resShapTopValUs), then
\textit{\resShapSecondFeatUs} (\resShapSecondValUs) and \textit{\resShapThirdFeatUs}
(\resShapThirdValUs). The two channels thus rely on distinct features whose
operating-condition leanings differ---the dominant AE feature is speed-tracking,
whereas the dominant US feature is the temperature-leaning 10--20~kHz mobility
(Figure~\ref{fig:marg_cond})---so SHAP is consistent with their separate-channel
structure. (The US band labels refer to the probe's heterodyned audio baseband, not
raw acoustic frequencies, so the contrast is one of operating-condition channel
rather than frequency.) Cross-model agreement for both sensors is summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:shap_agree}.
