\begin{abstract}
Inadequate lubrication causes an estimated 30--80\% of rolling-element bearing
failures, yet online, non-invasive lubrication condition monitoring (LCM) remains
unsolved: lubrication-relevant signal content is weak and strongly confounded with
operating conditions. This paper presents a controlled dual-channel study in which
wideband acoustic emission (AE) and heterodyned passive ultrasound (US) are acquired
simultaneously from a ball bearing across staircase speed sweeps
(\resRpmMinMeas--\resRpmMaxMeas~rpm) and 13 temperature blocks
(\resTempMinMeas--\resTempMaxMeas~\si{\celsius}), spanning the boundary, mixed, and
full-film regimes, with the ISO~281 viscosity ratio $\kappa$ as a continuous,
physics-grounded regression target. Because $\kappa$ is determined by the operating
point, the central methodological question is what a feature--$\kappa$ regression
actually senses: a two-stage decomposition shows that acoustically inferred
operating points alone recover $R^2 = \resTwoStageRsq$ of the direct model's
\resHOrsqLgbAe, while within-speed-step analysis isolates a genuine
temperature-mediated lubrication channel (\resWithinStepFeat,
$\rho = \resWithinStepRhoHC$ at fixed drive state). Evaluation uses visit-grouped
data splits that remove the near-duplicate leakage of windowed acquisition;
recorded content above the AE sensor's 1~MHz specification is excluded from
analysis. Under this protocol, gradient boosting attains hold-out
$R^2 = \resHOrsqLgbAe$ (AE) and \resHOrsqLgbUs{} (US); fusing the channels raises
performance to \resHOrsqLgbComb{} ($\Delta\text{RMSE} = \resDrmseAeComb$,
significant on all tests), answering the long-argued but previously untested
question of AE--US complementarity. All results derive from repeated grouped
cross-validation with corrected significance tests.
\end{abstract}
