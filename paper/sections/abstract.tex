\begin{abstract}
This paper investigates whether acoustic emission (AE) and passive ultrasound
(US) sensing provide complementary information about the lubrication condition of
rolling-element bearings, and what such measurements actually capture. Wideband
AE and heterodyned passive US are acquired simultaneously from a single ball
bearing on an instrumented test rig, while the operating condition is varied over
a staircase of rotational speeds repeated across a sequence of temperature
blocks, driving the lubricant through the boundary, mixed, and full-film regimes.
The ISO~281 viscosity ratio $\kappa$, computed from the measured speed and
temperature, serves as a continuous reference for oil-film adequacy, and
frequency-band features from both channels are regressed onto $\kappa$ with a
regularised linear model and a gradient-boosted tree ensemble. To obtain
deployment-representative estimates, the models are evaluated under an
acquisition-grouped protocol that keeps closely related measurement segments from
being split across training and evaluation.

Both channels predict $\kappa$ accurately, and combining them improves on either
alone---the first quantitative confirmation of the AE--US complementarity that had
previously only been argued. A two-stage analysis shows, however, that almost all
of this predictive performance can be recovered from the operating point that the
sensors infer acoustically: within this dataset the acoustic $\kappa$-regression
behaves as an operating-point soft sensor rather than a direct measurement of the
lubricant film. The two channels are complementary because they encode partly
different aspects of the operating point, ultrasound resolving temperature more
strongly than acoustic emission. These findings clarify what acoustic
$\kappa$-regression does and does not demonstrate about lubrication sensing, and
indicate that detecting lubrication changes not caused by the operating condition
remains an open problem.
\end{abstract}
