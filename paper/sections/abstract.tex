\begin{abstract}
This paper investigates whether acoustic emission (AE) and passive ultrasound (US) provide complementary information about the lubrication condition of rolling-element bearings. Wideband AE and heterodyned passive US are acquired simultaneously and time-synchronised from a single ball bearing on an instrumented test rig, while the operating condition is varied over a staircase of rotational speeds repeated across a sequence of temperature blocks, driving the lubricant through the boundary, mixed, and full-film regimes. The ISO~281 viscosity ratio $\kappa$, computed from the operating condition, serves as a continuous reference for lubrication adequacy. Frequency sub-band features from both channels are regressed onto $\kappa$ with a regularised linear model and a gradient-boosted tree ensemble, evaluated under an acquisition-grouped protocol that prevents leakage of near-duplicate segments across the training--evaluation boundary. Either channel alone supports accurate $\kappa$ regression. Combining them significantly outperforms either alone, for both model families under paired group-level tests, providing, to the authors' knowledge, the first quantitative demonstration of AE--US complementarity. This complementarity is supported with a correlation analysis and by grouped SHAP attribution. These show that the channels encode partly distinct operating-condition information, with US associated with temperature substantially more strongly than AE, and that features from both channels are important for $\kappa$ regression.
\end{abstract}
