\section{Feature Analysis}
\label{sec:features}
%% ============================================================================

In this section, the correlation of features with $\kappa$ and inter-feature redundancy are analysed to identify the most informative features for modelling.
The procedure is as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Spearman and Pearson correlation ranking,
    \item Variation inflation factor calculation,
    \item feature selection based upon correlation and VIF,
    \item dimensionality reduction and visualization.
\end{itemize}

The top AE features are drawn overwhelmingly from the 1--2~MHz and 500~kHz--1~MHz sub-bands, with sub-band complexity ($|\rho| = 0.891$) and mobility ($|\rho| = 0.841$) heading the ranking.
Broadband AE features rank lower than their sub-band counterparts, indicating that the frequency-specific content of the high-frequency AE signal carries more monotonic information about $\kappa$ than the full-band aggregate~\cite{renhart_ae_2021, zhang_coupled_2026}.
For the US channel, amplitude features (spectral mean, peak, RMS, std) dominate at $|\rho| \approx 0.60$--$0.66$; the maximum US correlation ($|\rho| = 0.663$) is substantially lower than the top AE correlation ($|\rho| = 0.891$).

\subsection{Operating-condition confounding}
\label{subsec:confound}

The raw Spearman correlation between broadband signal energy (as a proxy for all amplitude features) and $\kappa$ is substantially driven by the shared dependence on rotational speed and temperature.
Quantitatively, RPM has a Spearman correlation of $\rho = +0.62$ with $\kappa$ and temperature has $\rho = -0.68$.
Higher RPM reduces the required viscosity $\nureq$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:nu1}) and therefore increases $\kappa$, while simultaneously increasing signal energy through more frequent contact events per second.
Higher temperature reduces the actual viscosity $\nuop$ and therefore reduces $\kappa$, while also affecting material damping and structural dynamics.

As a consequence, amplitude-dominated features such as RMS, variance, and peak --- which rank among the top features by raw Spearman correlation --- are partially acting as indirect proxies for rotational speed rather than as direct indicators of lubrication film adequacy~\cite{hou_insitu_2025, zhang_coupled_2026, miettinen_analysis_2001}. This confounding is an inherent property of any single-operating-point sensor system without an explicit operating condition normalisation step.
