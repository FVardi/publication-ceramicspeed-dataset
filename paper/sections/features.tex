\section{Feature Analysis}
\label{sec:features}
%% ============================================================================

This section reduces the \resNcandAe~AE and \resNcandUs~US candidate features
(Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}) to compact, non-redundant subsets for modelling, and
characterises what the surviving features respond to. The analysis proceeds in two
selection stages -- a relevance filter against $\kappa$ followed by a redundancy
reduction -- after which the operating-condition dependence and low-dimensional
structure of the retained sets are examined.

\subsection{Two-stage feature selection}
\label{subsec:selection}

\textbf{Stage 1 -- relevance.} Each candidate feature is ranked by its absolute
Spearman ($|\rho|$, monotonic association) and Pearson ($|r|$, linear association)
correlation with $\kappa$ across all sweeps. Features must satisfy both
$|\rho| \geq 0.1$ and $|r| \geq 0.1$ to be retained, removing features carrying
essentially no monotonic or linear information about the target.

\textbf{Stage 2 -- redundancy.} Among the survivors, redundancy is reduced by a
greedy pairwise procedure. Features are visited in order of increasing relevance
($|\rho|$ with $\kappa$); whenever two features are inter-correlated at
$|\rho| \geq 0.90$ and the candidate for removal also has a variance inflation
factor $\text{VIF} \geq 10$, the member of the pair with the higher relevance is
kept and the other is dropped. The VIF thus acts as a qualifier for pairwise
removal rather than as a stand-alone criterion: no feature is removed for high VIF
alone, and a retained feature may still exhibit $\text{VIF} > 10$ when its
collinearity is distributed across several features rather than concentrated in a
single partner.

\subsection{Operating-condition dependence}
\label{subsec:confound}

The raw correlation between signal features and $\kappa$ is partially driven by the
shared dependence on rotational speed and temperature: RPM has a Spearman correlation
of $\rho = \resRhoRpmKappa$ with $\kappa$ and temperature has
$\rho = \resRhoTempKappa$.
Higher RPM reduces the required viscosity $\nureq$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:nu1}) and therefore
increases $\kappa$, while simultaneously increasing signal energy through more
frequent contact events per second. Higher temperature reduces the actual viscosity
$\nuop$ and therefore reduces $\kappa$, while also affecting material damping and
structural dynamics.

As a consequence, amplitude features such as RMS are partially acting as indirect
proxies for rotational speed rather than as direct indicators of lubrication film
adequacy~\cite{hou_insitu_2025, zhang_coupled_2026, miettinen_analysis_2001}.
This confounding is inherent to any single-operating-point sensor system without an
explicit operating-condition normalisation step. Because $\kappa$ is fully determined
by the operating point (Section~\ref{subsec:kappa}), a model given measured RPM and
temperature directly would predict $\kappa$ near-perfectly by construction; the
contribution of the acoustic features is to recover this information without
tachometer or thermometer access.
This proxy structure is quantified directly. A LightGBM regressor trained on the
retained AE features predicts RPM with $R^2 = \resProxyRpmRsq$
(RMSE \resProxyRpmRmse~rpm) and temperature with $R^2 = \resProxyTempRsq$
(RMSE \resProxyTempRmse~\si{\celsius}) on the hold-out set. Feeding these predicted
operating points through Eqs.~\eqref{eq:kappa}--\eqref{eq:nu1} yields a two-stage
$\kappa$ estimate with $R^2 = \resTwoStageRsq$ (RMSE \resTwoStageRmse), nearly
matching the direct features-to-$\kappa$ model ($R^2 = \resHOrsqLgbAe$,
RMSE \resHOrmseLgbAe). The direct model is therefore well described as an acoustic
operating-point soft sensor combined with the ISO~281 mapping, with only a marginal
contribution beyond it.

The features are not, however, mere speed trackers. Within a fixed staircase RPM
step the drive state is approximately constant while the lubricant viscosity varies
across the 13 temperature blocks; in this setting the strongest feature
(1--2~MHz complexity) retains a median within-step Spearman correlation of
$\resWithinStepRhoHC$ with temperature (negative in 91\% of steps, i.e.\ tracking
$\kappa$; Figure~\ref{fig:within_step}), while remaining constant during the
stationary heater-driven warm-up. The features therefore sense both $\kappa$
channels---speed and temperature---through the rotating bearing, rather than
reading either operating quantity directly.
