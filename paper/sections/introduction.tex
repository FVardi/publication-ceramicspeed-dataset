\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ============================================================================

% Importance of bearing lubrication
Rolling element bearings are used throughout industrial machinery, and how well
they run depends heavily on whether they are properly lubricated. When
lubrication fails---through starvation, degraded viscosity, or
contamination---metal surfaces inside the bearing come into direct contact, wear
accelerates, and the bearing eventually fails. Lubrication problems are
estimated to cause between 30\% and 80\% of all rolling-bearing
failures~\cite{jakobsen_detecting_2023,dai_situ_2024,nassef_impact_2022}.

% Why LCM lags behind vibration-based CM, and why it is hard (incl. the confound)
Despite this, monitoring the lubrication condition of a running bearing has
received far less attention than monitoring it for mechanical faults. Vibration-based
fault detection and remaining-useful-life estimation are mature, widely deployed
technologies, across both vibration and acoustic-emission
sensing~\cite{wang_phm_2018,shang_comprehensive_2024,wang_fingerprint_2023}.
Lubrication condition monitoring (LCM) is harder, for two reasons. First, the
acoustic and vibration signatures of a thinning oil film are weaker and less
distinctive than the sharp impulses produced by a crack or a spall, and they are
easily masked by changes in speed and load. This entanglement with the operating
point is a recognised problem across bearing diagnostics generally---it has, for
instance, motivated a substantial recent literature on fault diagnosis under
varying and unseen working
conditions~\cite{marticorena_cage_2022,shang_comprehensive_2024,zhang_coupled_2026,pang_smia_2024,hua_dife_2025,jiang_mechanism_2025}.
Second, for lubrication specifically the problem is sharper still: the quantity
to be estimated, the ISO~281 viscosity ratio $\kappa$ (introduced below), is
itself a known function of speed and temperature. A model can therefore appear
to predict lubrication condition while secretly just reading off the operating
point---a confound this paper addresses directly with an explicit diagnostic
decomposition (Section~\ref{subsec:confound}). Solving this is worthwhile: an LCM
system that works would let lubricant be applied only when actually needed,
instead of on a fixed schedule that systematically under- or
over-lubricates~\cite{wakiru_review_2019}.

% Limitations of conventional CM for LCM
Several existing sensing options each have their own limits. Classical vibration
analysis looks for the stable, periodic signatures of structural damage such as
raceway spalling, and is poorly matched to the gradual, non-periodic process of
film formation and breakdown~\cite{shang_comprehensive_2024,cornel_condition_2021};
recent data-driven methods do recover some lubrication-state information from
vibration, typically from high-frequency broadband energy rather than from
characteristic frequencies~\cite{zhang_interpretable_2026}. Oil analysis and
temperature monitoring add complementary information, but both are indirect:
they are delayed, or they require physical access to the lubricant reservoir,
and neither directly tracks the instantaneous lubrication regime during
operation~\cite{wakiru_review_2019,sun_online_2021}.

% Passive AE and US as practical sensing routes (detail deferred to Background)
Two passive, non-invasive techniques are practically attractive instead:
acoustic emission (AE) and passive ultrasound (US). Neither requires modifying
the bearing or accessing the lubricant, and both can be retrofitted to bearings
already in service. AE captures the broadband stress waves released by asperity
contact and microcracking; passive US underlies the threshold-based
relubrication tools already used in industry, where a rise in broadband
ultrasonic level above a baseline triggers
relubrication~\cite{lineham_ultrasonic_2008,jakobsen_detecting_2023,jakobsen_low_2024}.
Their physical origins are detailed in Section~\ref{sec:background}. Despite the
practical maturity of threshold-based US and the demonstrated usefulness of AE,
the two channels' signal-processing behaviour has not been characterised
simultaneously within a single regression framework against a physics-grounded
lubrication reference.

% Literature review: AE sensitivity to lubrication regime + signal-processing/physical models
AE's sensitivity to lubrication regime is well established. AE features have
been shown to distinguish boundary, mixed, and full-film regimes and to respond
to base-oil viscosity and grease thickener content~\cite{miettinen_analysis_2001},
to outperform accelerometers for detecting grease
contamination~\cite{tandon_ae_2006}---the scenario closest to the present
study---and, combined with vibration monitoring via envelope
demodulation~\cite{yoshioka_compound_2009}, to detect lubrication-sensitive
surface damage up to eight hours earlier than vibration
alone~\cite{cornel_condition_2021}. On the signal-processing side, octave
filter-bank and spectral-correlation features extracted from AE have been shown
to track lubrication regime even as load
varies~\cite{renhart_ae_2021,marticorena_cage_2022}, and physically grounded
models now tie the AE signature directly to elastohydrodynamic film thickness
and asperity-contact rate~\cite{liu_combined_2021,zhang_coupled_2026}. Hou and
Zhang~\cite{hou_insitu_2025} derived amplitude- and energy-centre features that
classify lubrication status across speeds and viscosity levels, though without
a continuous regression target and with no variation of temperature. For the
passive-ultrasound channel specifically, Jakobsen et
al.~\cite{jakobsen_detecting_2023,jakobsen_low_2024} showed that broadband
acoustic features carry measurable information about $\kappa$---the target this
paper also uses.

% Kappa as a continuous, physics-grounded target
Most data-driven LCM work, however, predicts discrete fault labels or
qualitative lubrication states rather than a continuous, physically meaningful
quantity~\cite{zhang_interpretable_2026}. The ISO~281 viscosity ratio $\kappa$
avoids this limitation. It is the ratio between the lubricant's actual
kinematic viscosity at the running temperature and the minimum viscosity
required to form a full elastohydrodynamic (EHL) film at the current speed and
bearing geometry, mapping the instantaneous operating point onto a standardised
lubrication-adequacy index (Section~\ref{sec:background}). A model trained to
predict $\kappa$ must implicitly capture the joint effect of speed, temperature,
and lubricant properties, and its output can be read directly against
established lubrication-regime boundaries. Despite this advantage, $\kappa$ has
seldom been used as a regression target in acoustic condition monitoring.

% Jakobsen relation and the consolidated gap
The clearest precedent is the work of Jakobsen et
al.~\cite{jakobsen_detecting_2023,jakobsen_low_2024}, who were first to regress
$\kappa$ from passive bearing acoustics, using a low-cost MEMS microphone and
accelerometer with LASSO and neural-network models. This study adopts their
$\kappa$ target and extends it in three respects: it adds a wideband AE channel,
analysed up to the \SI{1}{MHz} limit of the AE coupler's band-pass, acquired
simultaneously with the passive US channel; it extracts frequency sub-band
features from both signals; and it tests directly whether the two channels are
complementary. That last question carries real practical weight---a second
sensor adds cost and complexity and is justified only if it supplies
non-redundant information---but it has so far only been argued on physical
grounds in reviews~\cite{shang_comprehensive_2024,wang_advanced_2024}, never
quantified experimentally. No prior study has simultaneously acquired AE and
passive US from a single ball bearing and assessed their joint value for
$\kappa$ regression.

% Scope and generalizability
This study deliberately fixes the bearing, lubricant, and load, varying only
rotational speed and temperature. Holding the mechanical configuration constant
isolates the lubrication-dependent acoustic response from the bearing-to-bearing
variability and degradation processes that would otherwise confound it---a
necessary first step before asking whether AE and passive US carry
$\kappa$-relevant information at all. The resulting limits on generalisation
across bearing types, lubricants, and load regimes are genuine, but not
addressed here.

\subsection*{Contributions}

This paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A confound-decomposition methodology for acoustic LCM.} Because
  $\kappa$ is determined by the operating point, any feature--$\kappa$ regression
  risks acting as an operating-point soft sensor. A two-stage decomposition
  (features $\rightarrow$ RPM/temperature $\rightarrow$ ISO~281 mapping, recovering
  $R^2 = \resTwoStageRsq$ of the direct model's \resHOrsqLgbAe) quantifies the proxy
  share, while within-RPM-step correlation analysis of \resWithinStepFeat{}
  (median $\rho = \resWithinStepRhoHC$ with temperature at fixed drive state)
  isolates the genuine lubrication-channel sensitivity.
  \item \textbf{The AE--US complementarity question is answered quantitatively.}
  This is the first study to simultaneously acquire wideband AE and heterodyned
  passive US from a single ball bearing and assess their joint value: fusing the
  channels reduces hold-out RMSE from \resHOrmseLgbAe{} to \resHOrmseLgbComb{}
  ($\Delta\text{RMSE} = \resDrmseAeComb$), significant under repeated grouped
  cross-validation and all hold-out tests, with the extended 20--100~kHz US band
  contributing selected features to the combined model. AE outperforms US on the
  hold-out set, while fold-level inference between the single channels is
  inconclusive under group-respecting cross-validation.  \item \textbf{A leakage-aware, instrumentation-honest evaluation protocol.}
  Recorded content above the \SI{1}{MHz} low-pass of the AE coupler (Kistler
  Type~5125C)---present in the digitised signal despite that hardware filter, and
  of undetermined origin---is excluded from analysis. Visit-grouped data splits remove the
  near-duplicate leakage inherent to windowed acquisition of slowly varying
  protocols. Under matched random-split protocols, performance equals prior
  $\kappa$-regression work; the stricter visit-grouped figures are, to the
  authors' knowledge, the first reported for this task.
  \item \textbf{A dual-channel dataset and reproducible evaluation protocol.}
  \resNsweeps{} sweeps spanning the boundary through full-film regimes
  ($\kappa \in [\resKappaMin,\,\resKappaMax]$) with an analytically derived ISO~281
  $\kappa$ reference, evaluated under repeated nested cross-validation
  (\resNouterScores{} outer scores) with Nadeau--Bengio-corrected and
  Holm--Bonferroni-adjusted inference.\todo{Align with final data availability
  decision (full deposit, features+code only, or on request).}
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:background} reviews the theory of $\kappa$ and the physical origins of AE and US signals.
Section~\ref{sec:setup} describes the experimental setup and data collection.
Section~\ref{sec:pipeline} presents the signal-processing and feature-extraction
pipeline, including the characterisation of the frequency sub-bands.
Section~\ref{sec:features} reports the feature analysis and the
operating-condition confound decomposition. Section~\ref{sec:modelling} presents
the regression modelling and statistical evaluation.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the findings, and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.
