\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%% ============================================================================

The experiments show that both acoustic channels support accurate $\kappa$
regression under a leakage-controlled protocol, that their combination
outperforms either channel alone, and that essentially all of this performance
is attributable to operating-point inference. The obtained results are
discussed as follows.

\subsection{The $\kappa$ regression is an operating-point soft sensor}
\label{subsec:disc_softsensor}

The two-stage decomposition (Section~\ref{subsubsec:decomposition}) shows that
the direct $\kappa$-regression performance is recovered almost entirely from
acoustically inferred operating points alone: for every channel, predicting RPM
and temperature from the features and passing the estimates through the ISO~281
mapping matches the direct regression to within $\approx$0.01~$R^2$. Since the
features recover rotational speed almost perfectly and temperature well, and
$\kappa$ is a deterministic function of the measured operating point, this outcome is mechanistically plausible; what the decomposition establishes is that there is no measurable performance contribution beyond it. The small positive AE residual is consistent with error
propagation in the two-stage temperature estimate and provides no evidence of lubrication-specific sensing. Within this dataset, the acoustic $\kappa$
regression is therefore best understood as an operating-point soft sensor
combined with the ISO~281 mapping.

This finding does not render the models useless. An acoustic soft sensor that
recovers speed, temperature, and thereby nominal lubrication adequacy from a
single externally mounted sensor---without tachometer or thermometer
access---may be operationally valuable, particularly for retrofit monitoring.
It does, however, change what the performance figures mean. High
$\kappa$-regression accuracy, in this study and in prior work including
Jakobsen et al.~\cite{jakobsen_detecting_2023}, should not by itself be read as
evidence that the sensors detect the state of the lubricant film beyond what
the operating point implies. Two consequences follow. First, generalisation to
operating regimes outside the training distribution is not supported by the
reported accuracy, since the learned mapping is anchored in the operating
point. Second, changes in lubrication adequacy that are \emph{not} caused by
the operating point---degradation, contamination, starvation, none of which
$\kappa$ encodes---are outside the demonstrated
capability, and detecting them with and online, non-invasive method remains an open problem.

\subsection{AE--US complementarity and channel encoding}
\label{subsec:disc_channels}

The fusion result is the most robust finding of the study: the combined feature
set outperforms both single channels, for both model families, in all four
group-level DM contrasts (Table~\ref{tab:inference}). The gain is modest in
magnitude but consistent, and it has a structural basis in the
operating-condition encoding of the two channels. Conditionally on speed, US
resolves temperature substantially better than AE
(Section~\ref{subsec:confound}, Table~\ref{tab:decomposition}), whereas both
channels track speed near-perfectly; the clustered SHAP results mirror this
separation, with the AE model led by a speed-tracking 500~kHz--1~MHz mobility
cluster and the US model drawing on temperature-leaning baseband features. The
two channels are thus complementary primarily because they encode partly
distinct components of the operating point---a quantified answer to the
complementarity question that has not previously been tested in the LCM
literature.

Which single channel is preferable proved to be model-family dependent: Elastic
Net favours US while LightGBM favours AE, both significantly. A plausible
explanation is that the temperature-mediated US structure is closer to linear
and is captured well by a regularised linear model, whereas the speed-dependent
AE structure is strongly non-linear and requires the tree ensemble. Channel
rankings obtained under a single model family should therefore be treated with
caution.

The comparison is further bounded by hardware. The AE sensor captures wideband
content up to \SI{1}{MHz}, dominated by verified stress-wave emission, whereas
the US probe down-mixes a narrow $\approx$38--40~kHz sensing band to an
audio-frequency baseband, within which the
10--20~kHz half carries the strongest correlations. The high-frequency
transient signature of individual contact events is largely absent from this
down-mixed envelope. Whether a wideband passive ultrasound sensor would supply
more independent information---and thus a larger fusion gain---remains an open
question.

\subsection{On the use of $\kappa$ as lubrication reference}
\label{subsec:disc_kappa}

The viscosity ratio is not measured but computed for each segment from the
measured operating point via the ISO~281 relations and the ASTM~D341 Walther
equation (Section~\ref{subsec:kappa}). It therefore inherits the assumptions of
those relations, notably the use of housing temperature as a proxy for the
elastohydrodynamic contact temperature and the assumption of nominally intact
lubricant. A further assumption is that lubricant supply is sufficient for fully
flooded inlet conditions: as noted in Section~\ref{subsec:kappa}, high rotational
speeds can induce starvation that reduces the actual film below $\kappa$'s
prediction even when the bulk lubricant is intact~\cite{poll_starved_2019}.
Whether the staircase protocol's higher speed steps induced starvation, and
whether the acoustic signals carry a starvation signature that the regression
attributes to the operating point rather than the film state, has not been
investigated. More fundamentally, because the target is deterministic in the
operating point, some degree of $\kappa$-regression success is guaranteed for
any feature set that is informative about the operating point. The
decomposition of Section~\ref{subsubsec:decomposition} quantifies this share
rather than leaving it implicit. Breaking the circularity requires an independent reference---a
film-thickness measurement~\cite{shang_comprehensive_2024}, electrical contact resistance, or an experiment in
which the lubricant state is varied independently of the operating point.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol and comparability with prior work}
\label{subsec:disc_protocol}

The acquisition-grouped, twin-merged evaluation removes the near-duplicate
leakage admitted by random splits over short measurement windows, and the
group-level DM tests avoid the potential significance inflation of window-level testing. The reported figures are consequently not directly
comparable to prior work evaluated under random splits, including
Jakobsen et al.~\cite{jakobsen_detecting_2023}; they are intended as more
conservative, deployment-representative estimates.

A caveat applies in the opposite direction as well. Because the
(RPM, temperature) space is densely and smoothly sampled, every held-out group
is surrounded by near-neighbour training points: the high $R^2$ reflects
\emph{interpolation} of the smooth $\kappa$(operating-point) surface, consistent
with the soft-sensor interpretation, rather than extrapolation to unseen
regimes. Same-operating-point leakage is controlled, but generalisation to
unsampled operating regimes is untested.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:disc_limits}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Single bearing, lubricant, and session.} All results are
    specific to the SYJ25 bearing, Keratech~22 oil, and one 14-hour
    experimental session. Generalisability to other bearing types, sizes,
    lubricants, or installations is unknown.
  \item \textbf{Heterodyned US channel.} The passive US probe down-mixes a
    narrow sensing band rather than recording wideband ultrasound; the
    US-channel results should not be equated with those of a wideband passive
    ultrasound sensor.
  \item \textbf{Skewed $\kappa$ coverage.} The sampled $\kappa$ distribution is
    concentrated in the boundary and mixed regimes, with full-film states
    ($\kappa > 1$) under-represented; extrapolation into the full-film regime should be treated with caution.
  \item \textbf{Model-derived $\kappa$ reference.} The regression target
    inherits the ISO~281 and Walther-equation assumptions and is deterministic
    in the operating point; no independent
    measurement of the lubrication state was available.
  \item \textbf{Interpolation, not extrapolation.} The grouped evaluation
    controls same-operating-point leakage but not regime novelty; performance
    on operating regimes outside the sampled staircase, or under independently
    varied lubricant state, is untested.
\end{enumerate}
