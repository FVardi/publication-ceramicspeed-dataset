\subsection{Sub-band decomposition}
\label{subsec:filter_bands}

The AE and US signals are decomposed into frequency sub-bands prior to feature
extraction, since the information on lubrication conditions contained in the signals
is expected to be localised in sub-bands rather than wholly contained in the broadband
signals~\cite{renhart_ae_2021, zhang_coupled_2026}. Adaptive decompositions such as symplectic geometry mode decomposition offer data-driven alternatives for isolating informative components~\cite{pan_sgmd_2019}; fixed, physically motivated bands are preferred here so that features remain directly comparable across sweeps and interpretable against the sensing physics.
For the AE channel, two sub-bands are extracted in addition to the broadband signal: 20--500~kHz and 500~kHz--1~MHz which contain bands of spectral energy which upon visual inspection of Figure~\ref{fig:spectrograms} vary with the operating conditions. Content above the
\SI{1}{MHz} coupler low-pass (Section~\ref{subsec:sensors}) is excluded from the
feature set. For the US channel, the signal spans the
probe's 0--100~kHz acquisition band, and three sub-bands are extracted in addition to the broadband signal: 0--10~kHz and 10--20~kHz within the nominal heterodyned baseband (Section~\ref{subsec:origins}), and 20--100~kHz capturing the above-baseband content
whose information value is assessed empirically.
The sub-bands are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:subbands}.
All decompositions use 5th-order Butterworth filters applied forward--backward
(\texttt{sosfiltfilt}), giving zero phase distortion and a 10th-order effective
magnitude response.

\subsection{Sub-band signal validation}
\label{subsec:band_validation}

The retained sub-bands carry bearing-generated content rather than drive EMI. The VFD injects a switching comb at $\approx$4.7~kHz and its harmonics (Section~\ref{subsec:rig}); these lie below the 20~kHz lower edge of the retained AE bands and so cannot masquerade as bearing signal within them. More directly, total band power rises with rotational speed in both bands---$\rho = \resRhoPowRpmLow$ for 20--500~kHz (at 45--55~\si{\celsius}) and $\rho = \resRhoPowRpmMid$ for 500~kHz--1~MHz (Figure~\ref{fig:band_power})---as expected for mechanically generated contact and rolling noise~\cite{miettinen_analysis_2001, zhang_coupled_2026}, rather than the fixed-frequency behaviour of an electromagnetic-interference source.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{band_power_vs_rpm.png}
\caption{Total power of the two retained AE sub-bands as a function of RPM at fixed temperature windows. Both bands rise with speed, consistent with mechanically generated emission.}
\label{fig:band_power}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Feature extraction}
\label{subsec:features_extract}

Features are computed from the voltage signal of each sweep--sensor pair, for the broadband signal and for each sub-band. In total 14 features are computed, comprising 8 time-domain and 6 frequency-domain statistics, as listed in Tables~\ref{tab:features} and~\ref{tab:features_freq}. The set is deliberately canonical: commonly used companions such as standard deviation, variance, peak, impulse factor, and RMS frequency are exact transforms or products of the retained features (e.g.\ $\mathrm{std}=\mathrm{RMS}$ for zero-mean band-filtered signals; $\mathrm{peak}=\mathrm{crest}\times\mathrm{RMS}$; $\mathrm{impulse}=\mathrm{crest}\times\mathrm{shape}$; $f_{\mathrm{RMS}}^2 = f_c^2 + \sigma_w^2$)\todo{What is this?} and are therefore omitted, so that no candidate feature is a deterministic function of another. The frequency-centroid information that RMS frequency would carry is instead represented by the retained Hjorth mobility, its time-domain analogue ($\text{mobility}\approx 2\pi f_{\mathrm{RMS}}$): computed from $\operatorname{Var}(x')/\operatorname{Var}(x)$ rather than from the spectral moments, it is not an exact function of $f_c$ and $\sigma_w$.\todo{Don't understand this} All retained features (without the specific sub-bands) have previously been applied to signals for bearing condition monitoring and LCM~\cite{jakobsen_detecting_2023,zhang_graph_2024}; the Hjorth parameters originate from EEG analysis~\cite{hjorth_eeg_1970}.

All frequency-domain features are computed from the one-sided FFT magnitude spectrum
of the sweep. The spectral skewness and kurtosis are standardised by
$\sigma_w^p\,\Sigma S$ and are therefore dimensionless and invariant to overall signal
amplitude, so that spectral-shape information is separated from the amplitude
information carried by RMS.

\subsection{Candidate feature sets}
\label{subsec:candidates}

Extracting the 14-feature set independently for the broadband signal and each
sub-band yields $14 \times 3 = \resNcandAe$~candidate AE features (broadband plus two
sub-bands) and $14 \times 4 = \resNcandUs$~candidate US features (broadband plus three
sub-bands) per sweep. Features are named by band and statistic
(e.g.\ \texttt{AE\_500-1000kHz\_\_mobility}); broadband features carry no band prefix.
These two candidate sets, and their union (the combined AE+US set,
$\resNcandAe + \resNcandUs$ features), define the three sensor configurations---AE-only,
US-only, and combined---evaluated in Sections~\ref{sec:features}--\ref{sec:modelling}.
The reduction of these candidate sets to compact, non-redundant subsets for modelling
is described in Section~\ref{sec:features}.
