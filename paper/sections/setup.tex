\subsection{Test rig and bearing}
\label{subsec:rig}

The test rig is a custom-built bearing test stand at Aarhus University (Herning, Denmark). A single deep-groove ball bearing (model SYJ25, bore \SI{25}{mm}, pitch diameter $d_{pw} = \SI{38.0}{mm}$, 9 steel rolling elements, dynamic load rating \SI{14000}{N}) is driven by a Klee T712-2 0.55kW variable-frequency electric motor controlled through a variable-frequency drive (VFD), which is contolled by a PID-controller based upon measurements with a reflective tache sensor. Temperature is increased byu a heating element mounted on the bearing pillow bloack and controlled by an Omron E5CC PID controller based upon measurements from type K temperature sensors mounted on the outer ring of the bearing. The bearing is loaded radially by a static weight of 60kg and lubricated with 0.5ml \todo{Check that this is correct} of Keratech~22 oil (kinematic viscosity $\nu_{40} = \SI{22}{cSt}$, $\nu_{100} = \SI{4.1}{cSt}$ at 40\si{\celsius} and 100\si{\celsius}, respectively) \todo{Do we need NLGI cosistency grade?}. VFD switching frequency is nominally 5kHz, and the harmonic artifacts from this are sought filtered out \todo{Explain the process and expected outcome.}

\subsection{Operating conditions}
\label{subsec:ops}

The experiment follows a protocol which varies the operating conditions through changes in rotational speed (rpm) and housing temperature (\si{\celsius}). This consists of a staircase sweep from 100 to 3000 RPM and back down to 100 RPM in steps of 100 RPM with a 60-second hold at each step. This staircase is repeated for each of 13 temperature blocks (40--100~\si{\celsius} in 5~\si{\celsius} increments). The block temperature set-point is changed between staircases rather than during the high-speed hold: after each downsweep the rig is held at the low-rpm bottom of the staircase ($\approx$100~rpm) while the housing is brought to the next set-point and allowed to stabilise thermally. The recorded telemetry contains no true standstill (RPM~$=0$) sweeps; the only near-stationary data is a single warm-up segment (37--43~\si{\celsius}) acquired before the first staircase, which is excluded from analysis (Section~\ref{subsec:sensors}).\todo{The original protocol described a 2-min RPM\,=\,0 standstill at each block start, but the telemetry shows the 12 set-point increases occurring at $\approx$140--365~rpm (mean $\approx$250~rpm). The measured operating conditions span \resRpmMinMeas--\resRpmMaxMeas~rpm and \resTempMinMeas--\resTempMaxMeas~\si{\celsius}; the measured temperature range extends below the commanded \SI{40}{\celsius} floor (down to $\approx$\SI{33}{\celsius}), which we tentatively attribute to the inherent undershoot and instability of the temperature control rather than to any commanded set-point.\todo{Confirm cause of the sub-40\,\si{\celsius} excursions (controller undershoot / cooling during the low-rpm holds); author unsure.}

The shared sample rate and DAQ setup ensures that AE and US signals share timestamps.

\textbf{Acquisition windows (sweeps).}
Data are acquired as short windows rather than continuously: every
$\sim$\resWindowIntervalS~s the oscilloscope captures a \resWindowMs~ms window
($N \approx 2.5 \times 10^{5}$ samples per channel) containing time-synchronous AE
and US waveforms, together with the mean RPM and housing temperature over the window.
Throughout this paper, one such window is referred to as a \emph{sweep}, and a sweep
is the unit of observation for all feature extraction, model training, and
evaluation. After excluding an initial stationary warm-up segment recorded before the first
staircase, the protocol yields \resNsweepsRaw{} raw sweeps, of which
\resNsweepsRemoved{} with measured RPM above the \SI{3000}{rpm} protocol maximum are
discarded, leaving \resNsweeps{} sweeps per channel (median \resSweepsPerHold{}
sweeps per staircase hold). The resulting $\kappa$ distribution spans
$[\resKappaMin,\, \resKappaMax]$ with mean \resKappaMean{}
(Figure~\ref{fig:op_conditions_distribution}).
