The rig uses a deep-groove ball bearing of model SYJ25 with \SI{25}{mm} bore width, pitch diameter $d_{pw} = \SI{38.0}{mm}$, 9 steel rolling elements, and dynamic load rating \SI{14000}{N}. The bearing is driven by a Klee T712-2 0.55kW variable-frequency electric motor controlled through a variable-frequency drive (VFD), which is controlled by a PID-controller based on measurements by an OGT5000 diffuse reflection sensor. The VFD switching frequency is nominally 5kHz, and the harmonic artifacts from this are sought filtered out \todo{Explain the filter and how effective it is expected to be.}. Temperature is increased by a heating element mounted on the bearing pillow block and controlled by an Omron E5CC PID controller based on measurements from a type K temperature sensor mounted on the outer ring of the bearing. A static weight of 60kg loads the bearing radially, and the actual load is measured by an FN2114 pedal load cell. The bearing is lubricated with 0.5ml \todo{Check that this is correct} of Keratech~22 oil (kinematic viscosity $\nu_{40} = \SI{22}{cSt}$, $\nu_{100} = \SI{4.1}{cSt}$ at 40\si{\celsius} and 100\si{\celsius}, respectively). AE signals are collected by a Kistler Piezotron Type~8152C1 wideband piezoelectric sensor bonded to the bearing housing\todo{How is it mounted?}. Its output is conditioned by a Kistler Type~5125C AE Piezotron coupler, which applies a Butterworth band-pass filter from \SI{50}{kHz} to \SI{1}{MHz} to the raw AE signal before it is digitised. This band-pass sets the \SI{1}{MHz} upper analysis limit used throughout the paper. US signals are collected by a UE Systems Ultra-trak 750 sensor \todo{How is it mounted?}. Synchronized sampling is performed by an Agilent MSO2024 oscilloscope at a frequency of 12.5MHz.

\subsection{Experimental protocol for varying $\kappa$}
\label{subsec:ops}

The experiment follows a protocol which varies the nominal operating conditions through changes in rotational speed (RPM) and housing temperature (\si{\celsius}). This causes the viscosity ratio $\kappa$ of the bearing in the test rig to vary under controlled loading and bearing geometry. The nominal rotational speed follows a staircase sweep from 100 to 3000 RPM and back down to 100 RPM in steps of 100 RPM with a minimum 60-second hold at each step. This staircase is repeated for 13 temperature blocks. The nominal temperature is increased in coherent blocks from 40 to 100~\si{\celsius} in 5~\si{\celsius} increments. After each RPM downsweep from 3000 RPM to 100 RPM, the housing is brought to the next temperature set-point before the next RPM staircase sweep begins. Radial load is applied with a static dead-weight, and held constant throughout the experiment at approximately \SI{61}{kg}. The hold at the \SI{3000}{rpm} staircase peak is substantially longer than the ordinary 60-second steps (approximately 20~minutes), so this operating point is over-represented in the data: it accounts for roughly a third of all sweeps.\todo{Confirm why the 3000~rpm peak is held for $\sim$20~min (the temperature set-point is not changed there).} The total duration of the experiment is approximately 14 hours.

The lubrication conditions in the bearing are not expected to change during the experiment in other ways than what is entailed by rpm and temperature and the effect of these upon the lubricant. It is not expected or assumed that any degradation, starvation or contamination of the lubricant happens.

\subsection{Acquisition segments and measured operating conditions}
Data are acquired as short segments rather than continuously: every $\sim$\resWindowIntervalS~s the oscilloscope captures a \resWindowMs~ms window
($N \approx 2.5 \times 10^{5}$ samples per channel) containing time-synchronous AE and US waveforms, together with the RPM and housing temperature at the segment sampling time. Throughout this paper, one such segment serves as the unit of observation for all feature extraction, model training, and evaluation.

Since the operating conditions are maintained through a control loop, they vary from the nominal conditions specified by the protocol. The measured operating conditions span \resRpmMinMeas--\resRpmMaxMeas~rpm and \resTempMinMeas--\resTempMaxMeas~\si{\celsius}; the measured temperature range extends below the commanded \SI{40}{\celsius} floor (down to $\approx$\SI{33}{\celsius}), which is tentatively attributed to instability of the temperature control rather than to any commanded set-point. During the last temperature block, the speed recordings at the top of the speed staircase were corrupted and are therefore discarded.

After excluding an initial stationary warm-up segment recorded before the first staircase and \resNsweepsRemoved{} segments with measured RPM above the \SI{3000}{rpm}, \resNsweeps{} segments per channel remain (median \resSweepsPerHold{} segments per staircase hold). The measured operating conditions space and the distribution of calculated $\kappa$ values for the remaining segments are seen in Figure~\ref{fig:op_conditions_distribution}. The measured operating conditions over time, ordered by acquisition time are seen in Figure~\ref{fig:op_conditions_timeseries}. The $\kappa$ value for each segment is computed according to Equations~\eqref{eq:kappa} and \eqref{eq:nu1} using the per-segment measured RPM and temperature, with \nuop{} evaluated via the ASTM~D341 Walther equation. The $\kappa$ values range from adequate ($\kappa\approx1.6$) to insufficient ($\kappa\approx0.05$).
