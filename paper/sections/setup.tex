\subsection{Test rig and bearing}

$d_{pw} = \SI{38.0}{mm}$, 9 chrome steel rolling elements, dynamic load rating
\SI{14000}{N}) is driven by a variable-frequency electric motor controlled through a

\subsection{Sensor configuration and data acquisition}
\label{subsec:sensors}

Two sensors simultaneously acquired signals from the bearing housing through a shared oscilloscope-based data acquisition (DAQ) system:

\begin{description}
  \item[AE sensor.] A wideband piezoelectric AE sensor is bonded to the bearing housing.
    Sampling rate: \SI{1.6}{MHz} (nominally; effective bandwidth verified to
    \SI{800}{kHz} by the DAQ). Signal recorded to a digital oscilloscope with voltage
    range adapted to the signal level (\SI{500}{mV}--\SI{1}{V} per division depending
    on RPM; see Table~\ref{tab:ops}).
  \item[Passive UL sensor.] A heterodyned passive ultrasound probe is clamped to the bearing housing. The heterodyning process frequency-shifts the raw acoustic signal into the audible range ($<$\SI{10}{kHz}); the recorded signal therefore reflects low-frequency acoustic emissions rather than true wideband ultrasound. Voltage range: \SI{50}{mV} per division. Sampling rate: \SI{1.6}{MHz} (recorded at the same rate as the AE channel; effective content below \SI{10}{kHz}).
\end{description}
The oscilloscope samples at a shared rate of \SI{1.6}{MHz} across both channels, ensuring co-temporal acquisition of AE and passive UL signals. Data are acquired in individual time-series sweeps triggered at each operating condition.
Each sweep comprises a fixed number of samples from both channels recorded

\subsection{Operating conditions and $\kappa$ derivation}
\label{subsec:ops}

The experiment follows a protocol which varies the opreating conditions through rotational speed (rpm) and housing temperature (\si{\celsius}).

The test profile runs a staircase sweep from 100 to 3000 RPM and back down to 100 RPM, in steps of 100 RPM with a 60-second hold at each step. This is repeated for each of the 13 temperature blocks (40–100°C in 5°C increments), with a 2-minute standstill (RPM = 0) at the start of each block for thermal stabilisation.

The $\kappa$ value for each sweep is computed according to
Equations~\eqref{eq:kappa}--\eqref{eq:nu1} using the per-sweep measured RPM and
temperature, with $\nuop$ evaluated via the ASTM~D341 Walther viscosity--temperature
equation.
No separate reference measurement of film thickness is available; $\kappa$ is therefore
a model-derived quantity subject to the assumptions of the Hamrock--Dowson framework
and Walther's equation.

\textbf{Note on regime coverage.} The boundary lubrication regime ($\kappa < 0.5$)
dominates the dataset (60\%) because the bearing spends the largest fraction of the
speed ramp at lower speeds where the required viscosity $\nureq$ is high.
Full-film conditions ($\kappa \geq 1.0$) are reached only at higher shaft speeds and
account for 11\% of sweeps.
Model performance in the full-film regime is therefore evaluated on a smaller absolute
number of sweeps, which is a limitation discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{Dataset availability}

The complete dataset (HDF5 signal files, extracted features in Parquet format, and the
analysis pipeline scripts) will be made publicly available on Zenodo upon acceptance.
The Python analysis pipeline is configured via a single YAML file and is fully
reproducible from the raw HDF5 files.
